# UNIRIDE UNISTAY: Comprehensive Research and Feasibility Study

**Prepared for:** Project Founder
**Date:** November 2026
**Status:** Research & Planning Document (Pre-Development)
**Tagline:** Connecting Students to Rides and Stays


## 1. Executive Summary

UNIRIDE UNISTAY is a dual-service digital marketplace targeting university students in Tanzania. It combines on-demand motorcycle transportation (UNIRIDE) with student accommodation discovery and booking (UNISTAY) into a single platform. The core strategic insight is **contextual aggregation**: students already navigate both problems simultaneously (commuting to campus and securing housing near campus), yet no existing Tanzanian platform serves both needs with university-specific geographical filtering at its core.

**Key Findings:**

- **Market Size:** Tanzania had approximately **334,854 students** enrolled in higher education institutions in the 2023/24 academic year, with **54 higher education institutions** (33 universities) as of December 2024 . This represents a substantial, concentrated, and recurring user base.
- **Regulatory Reality:** Ride-hailing in Tanzania is regulated by **LATRA** under the Private Hire Services Regulations (2020, amended 2026). A ride-hailing operator license requires a functioning app with specific features, API integration with LATRA systems, and compliance with vehicle tracking and levy collection requirements . **This is a mandatory gate, not a suggestion.**
- **Data Protection:** The **Personal Data Protection Act (2022)** requires registration with the **PDPC** before collecting or processing personal data . Location data, student identity data, and payment information all fall under this regime.
- **Payment Handling:** UNIRIDE UNISTAY should **not** seek to become a payment system provider or e-money issuer. The minimum capital for e-money issuance is **TZS 500 million** . Instead, the platform should integrate with licensed mobile money providers (M-Pesa, Mixx by Yas, Airtel Money) as a merchant/aggregator.
- **Critical Risk:** The platform faces a **cold-start liquidity problem**. Without drivers, students cannot book rides; without students, drivers will not join. Without properties, students cannot book stays; without students, landlords will not list. The MVP must solve one side of each marketplace first, at one university, before expanding.

**Recommendation:** Launch a **web-first, mobile-responsive MVP** at **one university** (recommendation: University of Dodoma/UDOM due to its concentrated campus geography and large student population). Start with **UNISTAY** as the initial wedge (lower regulatory barrier, no real-time tracking requirement), build student用户 acquisition, then layer **UNIRIDE** once the user base and LATRA licensing are secured.


## 2. Project Background

Tanzania’s higher education sector has expanded significantly. Between 2022/23 and 2023/24, student enrollment grew by 6.4%, reaching 334,854 students across 54 institutions . The Higher Education Students’ Loans Board disbursed TZS 738.2 billion to 229,652 students in 2023/24, indicating a large population with at least partial financial liquidity .

Yet two persistent problems remain underserved:

1. **Transportation:** Bodaboda (motorcycle taxi) is the dominant mode of short-distance transport in Tanzanian cities and around campuses. However, hailing is informal, pricing is negotiated ad hoc, and safety is inconsistent. Existing ride-hailing platforms (Bolt, Uber, inDrive, Little) focus on general urban markets, not university-specific demand patterns or campus geography.
2. **Accommodation:** Students seeking hostels, rooms, and rental houses rely on word-of-mouth, physical notice boards, Facebook groups, and walking around neighborhoods. Listings are fragmented, often outdated, and lack verification. No dominant platform aggregates student accommodation near specific Tanzanian universities.

UNIRIDE UNISTAY proposes to solve both problems within one ecosystem, differentiated by **university-first filtering**: the student selects their university, and the platform surfaces drivers and properties relevant to that location.


## 3. Vision, Mission and Objectives

**Vision:** To become the default digital companion for Tanzanian university students’ daily logistics—moving between campus, home, and everything in between.

**Mission:** To provide verified, affordable, and convenient transportation and accommodation services tailored to the realities of university life in Tanzania.

**Objectives (First 18 Months):**
1. Launch a functional MVP at one university with at least 500 active student users.
2. Onboard at least 50 verified drivers and 100 verified property listings.
3. Achieve product-market fit signals: repeat ride bookings, accommodation inquiries converting to bookings, and positive user feedback.
4. Secure all mandatory regulatory approvals (LATRA ride-hailing license, PDPC registration).
5. Establish a sustainable commission-based revenue model with unit economics that can support expansion.


## 4. Problem Statement

**For Students:**
- Finding a reliable bodaboda near campus requires walking to a stage, negotiating price, and accepting safety risks without verification.
- Finding accommodation is chaotic: information is scattered across physical notices, social media, and brokers who charge informal fees. Listings often misrepresent conditions. Scams (fake listings, deposit theft) are common.
- Students have limited time, tight budgets, and are often new to the city—making them vulnerable to exploitation.

**For Drivers:**
- Bodaboda drivers around campuses operate informally. They lack a structured way to signal availability, receive pre-arranged requests, or build a reputation that commands consistent customers.
- Idle time is high; demand is unpredictable.

**For Property Owners:**
- Hostel and rental property owners struggle to fill vacancies efficiently. They rely on physical signage and word-of-mouth. Students outside the immediate area cannot discover their properties.
- Managing inquiries, viewings, and bookings is manual and time-consuming.


## 5. Proposed Solution

A single web platform (mobile-responsive, with native apps as a future phase) with two integrated modules:

| Module | Core Function | Primary Users |
|--------|--------------|---------------|
| **UNIRIDE** | On-demand motorcycle ride booking with driver discovery, fare estimation, and trip management | Students, Drivers |
| **UNISTAY** | Accommodation discovery and booking (hostels, rooms, rentals, apartments) with university-based filtering | Students, Property Owners |

**Key Differentiator:** The **university selector** is the primary navigation anchor. When a student selects “UDOM,” the platform filters both drivers and properties to that geographic zone. This is not a generic city-based filter—it reflects the reality that student mobility and housing demand are university-centric.

## 8. User Roles and Permissions

### 8.1 Students (Primary Users)
**Permissions:** Register/login; select university; manage profile; browse UNIRIDE drivers (public view); request rides; view fare estimates; track active ride (when available); manage ride history; browse UNISTAY properties; view property details; submit booking inquiries; manage accommodation inquiries; rate/review drivers and properties; receive notifications.

**Research-Recommended Additional Features:**
- **Saved addresses:** “Home,” “Campus,” “Library” for quick booking.
- **Fare splitting:** Split ride cost with another student going the same direction (future).
- **Safety check-in:** Share ride status with a trusted contact.
- **Verified student badge:** Require .ac.tz email or student ID upload for student pricing and trust.

### 8.2 Drivers (UNIRIDE Supply)
**Permissions:** Register/login; submit profile and documents; register vehicle; set operating university/zone; update availability status; receive ride requests; accept/reject; view pickup/destination; manage active trip; mark trip complete; view earnings; receive ratings.

**Availability Statuses (Recommended):**

| Status | Meaning | Display |
|--------|---------|---------|
| **IDLE** | Available for requests | Green badge |
| **ON ROAD** | Currently on a trip or en route to pickup | Busy/red badge |
| **OFFLINE** | Not accepting requests (driver-initiated) | Grey/hidden from active list |

**Critical Design Note:** The two-status system (IDLE/ON ROAD) is insufficient for reliability. Without an OFFLINE state, a driver who closes the app or loses connectivity would still appear “IDLE” to students. **Implement OFFLINE as a distinct state** that the driver controls or that is triggered by heartbeat timeout (e.g., no GPS ping for 5 minutes → auto-offline).

**Availability Accuracy Mechanisms:**
- **Heartbeat:** Driver app/web sends periodic location/status ping (every 30–60 seconds).
- **Auto-offline:** If no heartbeat received for X minutes, status changes to OFFLINE.
- **Request lock:** When a request is sent to a driver, status temporarily becomes “REQUESTED” (invisible to other students) until accepted/rejected/timeout.
- **Trip lock:** Once accepted and en route, status is ON ROAD.

### 8.3 Hostel Owners and Landlords (UNISTAY Supply)
**Permissions:** Register/login; create owner profile; register property (hostel, rental house, apartment); upload photos; set descriptions; set prices; specify location; associate with university/universities; register rooms/beds; manage availability; receive booking inquiries; accept/reject; manage reservations; update occupancy; view history.

**Property Type Distinction (Critical for Database Design):**

| Property Type | Bookable Unit | Example |
|---------------|---------------|---------|
| **Hostel** | Individual bed (within a room) or entire room | “Single room in 6-room hostel” |
| **Rental House** | Entire house (or individual rooms if shared) | “3-bedroom house for rent” |
| **Apartment** | Entire apartment unit | “2-bedroom apartment” |
| **Individual Room** | One room in a shared/private residence | “Room in family home” |

The database must support **hierarchical inventory**: Property → Room (optional) → Bed (optional). A hostel room may have 2–4 beds, each independently bookable. An apartment may have no room-level subdivision—the entire unit is one bookable entity.

### 8.4 System Administrator
**Permissions:** Full platform oversight. Manage users (all roles); verify drivers (documents, vehicle, license); verify properties; manage universities; oversee all bookings; manage payments/transactions; handle complaints/disputes; generate reports; configure platform settings.

**Recommended Additional Admin Features:**
- **Fraud flagging:** Flag suspicious listings, rapid-fire bookings, or review manipulation.
- **Document expiry tracking:** Driver licenses, vehicle insurance, property ownership documents—alert when expiring.
- **Audit log:** Every admin action logged with timestamp and admin ID.
- **Manual override:** Ability to suspend accounts, cancel bookings, issue refunds (within policy).


## 9. Functional Requirements

### 9.1 UNIRIDE (Transportation)
- FR-01: Student can set pickup and destination locations (map-based or text search).
- FR-02: System calculates distance and estimated fare.
- FR-03: System discovers available (IDLE) drivers within a configurable radius.
- FR-04: Student can send a ride request to a selected driver or broadcast to nearby drivers.

- FR-09: On completion, fare is recorded and student can rate driver.
- FR-10: Driver can view earnings history and current status.

- FR-14: Student can submit a booking inquiry with intended move-in date and duration.
- FR-15: Owner receives inquiry and can accept or reject.
- FR-16: On acceptance, both parties notified; booking is recorded.
- FR-17: Room/bed-level availability is tracked to prevent double booking.
- FR-18: Student can view booking history; owner can view occupancy calendar.

### 9.3 Platform-Wide
- FR-19: University selector persists across sessions.
- FR-20: All listings (drivers, properties) are filtered by university zone by default.

## 12. University-Based Filtering (Core Technical Requirement)

### 12.1 Data Model for Universities and Zones

**Universities Table:**
- `id`, `name`, `short_name`, `city`, `region`, `latitude`, `longitude`, `status`

**Service Zones (Critical):**
A university is not a point—it is an area. Drivers and properties serve zones, not just a single coordinate.

**Zone Model:**
- Each university has one or more **service zones** (polygons or radius-based).
- A driver registers their operating zone(s): “I operate around UDOM.”
- A property is associated with the nearest university zone(s): “This hostel serves UDOM and St. John’s.”
- Zones can overlap; a driver or property can serve multiple universities.

**Recommended Implementation (MVP):**
- **Radius-based zones** for simplicity: University has center point + default radius (e.g., 5 km for drivers, 10 km for properties).
- Store `university_id` on driver and property records as a foreign key, plus optional `latitude`/`longitude` for distance calculations.
- **Query:** When student selects UDOM, fetch all drivers where `university_id = UDOM` AND `status = IDLE`. For properties, fetch where `university_id = UDOM` OR the property is within X km of UDOM center.

**Future Enhancement:** PostGIS or MySQL spatial functions for polygon-based zones and precise distance queries.

### 12.2 GPS and Distance

- Use browser Geolocation API (with user permission) to determine student’s current location for “near me” sorting.
- **Haversine formula** or database spatial function for distance between two points.
- Map provider options for Tanzania: **Google Maps** (reliable, paid after free tier), **OpenStreetMap + Leaflet** (free, requires more development), **Mapbox** (paid, good performance). **Recommendation for MVP:** Start with Leaflet + OpenStreetMap for zero cost; migrate to Google Maps when revenue supports it and precise traffic/ETA is needed.

### 12.3 Dynamic Filtering Logic

```
Student selects university → 
  Session stores university_id → 
    UNIRIDE query: SELECT * FROM drivers WHERE university_id = ? AND status = 'IDLE' → 
    UNISTAY query: SELECT * FROM properties WHERE university_id = ? OR distance(property, university) < 10km →
      Render results
```


## 13. UNIRIDE Transportation System

### 13.1 Ride Lifecycle (Aligned with LATRA Requirements)

LATRA’s Ride Hailing Service Operator License Guide specifies required app functionality :

| LATRA Requirement | Implementation |
|-------------------|----------------|
| Customer registration with OTP | Phone number + OTP verification |
| Vehicle registration details | Driver vehicle form with plate, make, model, year |
| Driver details (name, photo) | Driver profile with photo upload |
| Request ride + accurate locations | Map picker for pickup/destination |
| Real-time estimate (driver to pickup) | ETA calculation based on distance |
| Vehicle movement display | Driver location updates via GPS |
| Trip cancellation (no charge if not started) | Cancellation policy with state-based charging |
| Emergency contact | Panic button / emergency contact field |
| Trip ending + location-based charge | Fare calculated on trip completion at destination |
| Receipt (start, end, fares, time) | Digital receipt generated per trip |
| Notifications (arrival, start, end) | In-app + push notifications |
| API integration with LATRA | Required before license issuance  |
| Vehicle tracking device | Hardware requirement for vehicles  |
| Platform calculates/collects/remits LATRA levies | Levy calculation built into fare  |

**Critical Note:** LATRA requires ride-sharing vehicles to be fitted with a **functioning vehicle tracking device authorized by the Authority** . This is a hardware requirement for drivers, not a software feature. UNIRIDE must either (a) require drivers to install LATRA-approved devices, or (b) confirm with LATRA whether smartphone GPS suffices for motorcycle bodaboda operations. **This requires direct clarification with LATRA before launch.**

### 13.2 Fare Model (Illustrative, Not Prescribed)

LATRA regulates fares for ride-hailing services. UNIRIDE cannot independently set fares without reference to LATRA’s fare framework. The platform must:
- Display fare estimates to students before booking.
- Calculate fares based on distance/time consistent with LATRA regulations.
- Collect and remit LATRA levies .

**Assumption for Planning:** A base fare + per-kilometer rate + time component, with UNIRIDE commission deducted from driver earnings. Exact rates must be confirmed with LATRA.

## 14. UNISTAY Accommodation System

### 14.1 Property Inventory Model

The core challenge is representing different property types with a unified booking logic.

**Entity Hierarchy:**

```
Property (hostel, house, apartment, room)
  ├── Room (optional: a hostel has rooms; an apartment may not)
  │     └── Bed (optional: a hostel room may have multiple beds)
  └── BookableUnit (the thing a student actually books)
```

**BookableUnit is the key abstraction:**
- For a **hostel bed**: BookableUnit = Bed ID.
- For a **hostel room** (single occupancy): BookableUnit = Room ID.
- For an **apartment**: BookableUnit = Property ID (entire unit).
- For a **rental house**: BookableUnit = Property ID or Room ID if shared.

**Availability logic:** A BookableUnit has a calendar of occupied dates. A booking request checks overlap before confirmation.

### 14.2 Booking Flow (MVP = Inquiry-Based, Not Instant Booking)

For the initial release, **do not implement instant booking with payment**. Instead:
1. Student views property and submits an **inquiry** (“I’m interested, move-in date X, duration Y”).
2. Owner receives inquiry and responds (accept/decline/request viewing).
3. If accepted, both parties coordinate offline for payment and move-in.

**Why?**
- Instant booking requires payment integration, refund policies, and dispute resolution infrastructure.
- Trust is not yet established; owners may want to meet students.
- Avoids the complexity of double-booking prevention at launch while the team validates demand.

**Future Phase:** Instant booking with deposit payment, cancellation policy, and automated refunds.

### 14.3 Double-Booking Prevention (When Instant Booking Arrives)

- Use **database transactions with row-level locking** on the BookableUnit during booking confirmation.
- Check for overlapping date ranges: `EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM bookings WHERE unit_id = ? AND status IN ('confirmed','pending') AND date_range_overlaps(?, ?))`.
- If overlap exists, reject booking and suggest alternatives.
- Implement a **hold period**: when a student initiates booking, the unit is soft-locked for 15 minutes while payment completes.

### 15.3 Accommodation Market

Student accommodation in Tanzania is largely informal:
- **University-owned hostels:** Limited capacity; often insufficient for student population.

**Gap:** A verified, university-filtered accommodation platform with student-relevant filters (price, distance to campus, room type, availability date) does not exist at scale.

## 16. Competitor Analysis (Detailed)

**Note:** Detailed competitor financials, user numbers, and internal metrics are not publicly available for Tanzanian operations. The analysis below is based on observable market positioning and publicly stated features.

### 16.1 Bolt (Primary Ride-Hailing Competitor)

**Services:** Car and bodaboda ride-hailing, food delivery (Bolt Food), scooter rental (some markets).
**Target Market:** General urban population.
**Business Model:** Commission per ride (typically 15–25% globally; Tanzania rate not publicly confirmed).
**Pricing Approach:** Algorithmic dynamic pricing.
**Strengths:** Large driver network, brand trust, integrated payments, mature technology, regulatory experience.
**Weaknesses (for UNIRIDE’s niche):** No university-specific filtering or context. A student at UDOM sees all Dodoma drivers, not just those near campus. No accommodation offering. No student-specific features or pricing.
**Technology:** Native mobile apps (iOS/Android), real-time GPS tracking, in-app payments.

**UNIRIDE’s Differentiation:** Not competing with Bolt city-wide. Competing for **university-zone density**. If UNIRIDE achieves 20+ active drivers at UDOM, a student’s wait time could be comparable to or better than Bolt, with the added benefit of drivers who know campus locations and student schedules.

### 16.2 inDrive

**Services:** Ride-hailing with passenger-set fare offers.
**Target Market:** Price-sensitive urban users.
**Strengths:** Negotiation appeals to budget-conscious students.
**Weaknesses:** No bodaboda focus at scale; no accommodation; no university context.

### 16.3 Facebook/WhatsApp Accommodation Groups

**Services:** Informal listings posted by owners and brokers.
**Strengths:** Free, already where students are.
**Weaknesses:** No verification, high scam prevalence, no structured search, no booking mechanism, listings go stale quickly.

**UNIRIDE UNISTAY’s Differentiation:** Verification (property documents checked), structured search (price, distance, room type), persistence (listings don’t disappear), and inquiry tracking.

### 16.4 Physical Brokers (Dalali)

**Services:** Offline matching of students to rooms.
**Strengths:** Local knowledge, personal trust.
**Weaknesses:** Fees (often one month’s rent), limited inventory visibility, no standardization.

**Opportunity:** Co-opt brokers as “verified agents” on the platform rather than competing against them. They bring inventory and local knowledge; the platform brings reach and structure.


## 17. Business Model

### 17.1 Revenue Options Evaluated

| Model | Pros | Cons | Verdict |
|-------|------|------|---------|
| **Ride commission (10–15%)** | Scales with usage; standard in ride-hailing | Drivers may resist; thin margins on short trips | **Primary (Phase 2, after UNIRIDE launch)** |
| **Accommodation booking fee (one-time, 5–10% of first month’s rent)** | Aligned with transaction value; owners accustomed to paying broker fees | Owners may bypass platform after first contact | **Primary (Phase 1, with UNISTAY)** |
| **Monthly driver subscription** | Predictable revenue; no per-ride friction | High churn if earnings don’t justify; barrier to driver acquisition | **Secondary (offer as alternative to commission)** |
| **Property-owner subscription (featured listings)** | Recurring; owners value visibility | Requires sufficient student traffic to be valuable | **Phase 3** |
| **Featured property listings (pay-per-boost)** | Low barrier; performance-based | Requires traffic volume; could degrade trust if not clearly labeled | **Phase 3** |
| **Advertising (local businesses)** | Passive | Dilutes focus; requires ad sales effort | **Not recommended for MVP** |
| **Premium student accounts** | Recurring | Students are price-sensitive; value proposition unclear | **Not recommended** |

### 17.2 Recommended Initial Revenue Model

**For UNISTAY (Launch Phase):**
- **Free for students** to browse and inquire.
- **Free for property owners** to list during the first 6 months (supply acquisition).
- **After 6 months or 100 listings (whichever comes first):** Introduce a **success fee** of 5–10% of the first month’s rent, charged to the owner upon confirmed booking.
- Rationale: Owners are accustomed to paying dalali fees (often 10–20% or one month’s rent). A 5–10% success fee is competitive and only charged when value is delivered.

**For UNIRIDE (Launch Phase):**
- **Commission per completed ride: 10–15%** of fare, deducted from driver earnings.
- **Alternative for drivers:** Flat daily/weekly subscription (e.g., TZS 2,000/day or TZS 10,000/week) with 0% commission. Offer both, let drivers choose.
- Rationale: Commission aligns platform success with driver success. Subscription appeals to drivers who do high volume.

### 17.3 Illustrative Financial Projections (Clearly Labeled Assumptions)

**Assumptions:**
- Launch at one university (UDOM).

## 19. Technology Stack Comparison

### 19.1 Frontend

| Option | Pros | Cons | Recommendation |
|--------|------|------|----------------|
| **Laravel Blade + Livewire/Alpine** | Fast development, SEO-friendly, single codebase, low complexity | Less “app-like” feel; requires page reloads (Livewire mitigates) | **✅ MVP Choice** |
| **React (SPA)** | Rich interactivity, component reuse, large ecosystem | Separate API needed; SEO challenges; higher complexity | Phase 2 (admin dashboard or mobile app) |
| **React Native / Expo** | Native mobile apps, code sharing | App store deployment, device testing, higher initial cost | Phase 3 (after web validation) |

**Recommendation:** Start with **Laravel Blade + Livewire** for the MVP. It allows a single developer to build a responsive, interactive web application quickly. Livewire provides dynamic behavior (driver status updates, booking forms) without a separate JavaScript SPA. When the platform needs native mobile apps, the Laravel API (built alongside Blade) can serve React Native.

### 19.2 Backend

| Option | Pros | Cons | Recommendation |
|--------|------|------|----------------|
| **Laravel (PHP)** | Mature, excellent documentation, built-in auth, ORM (Eloquent), queues, broadcasting, large Tanzania developer community | Slightly slower than Node for real-time | **✅ Recommended** |
| **Node.js (Express/Nest)** | Excellent for real-time (WebSockets), JavaScript full-stack | Requires more architecture decisions; smaller PHP-to-Node talent pool in Tanzania for quick hiring | Alternative if team is JS-fluent |

**Recommendation:** **Laravel**. The ecosystem (authentication, queues, notifications, database migrations, testing) accelerates solo development. Real-time features (driver location updates) can use Laravel Echo + Pusher/Ably (managed WebSockets) or Laravel Reverb (self-hosted WebSockets).

### 19.3 Database

| Option | Pros | Cons | Recommendation |
|--------|------|------|----------------|
| **PostgreSQL** | Robust, spatial data support (PostGIS), JSON fields, strong integrity | Slightly more complex setup than MySQL | **✅ Recommended** |
| **MySQL** | Widely used, simple, adequate for MVP | Spatial support less mature than PostGIS | Acceptable alternative |
| **Supabase** | Managed Postgres + auth + realtime + storage | Vendor lock-in; pricing scales with usage | Not recommended as primary; consider for rapid prototype |

**Recommendation:** **PostgreSQL**. Even if spatial queries are not used in MVP (radius search can use Haversine in application code), PostgreSQL’s data integrity and future PostGIS capability make it the better long-term choice. Use a managed service (e.g., DigitalOcean Managed PostgreSQL, AWS RDS) for backups and maintenance.

### 19.4 Additional Infrastructure

| Component | Recommendation | Rationale |
|-----------|----------------|-----------|
| **Authentication** | Laravel Breeze/Fortify + phone OTP (via SMS provider) | Phone number is the primary identity in Tanzania; email optional |
| **Image Storage** | Cloudflare R2 or AWS S3 | Property photos can be large; object storage is cheaper than server disk |
| **Maps** | Leaflet + OpenStreetMap (MVP); Google Maps (later) | Zero cost initially; upgrade when revenue supports |
| **Real-time** | Laravel Reverb (self-hosted) or Pusher (managed) | Reverb is free and Laravel-native; Pusher is reliable but paid |
| **Push Notifications** | Firebase Cloud Messaging (FCM) | Free, cross-platform (web + future mobile) |

| **Payments** | M-Pesa (Vodacom), Mixx by Yas, Airtel Money via aggregator (e.g., Selcom, Flutterwave) | Do not build payment processing; integrate as merchant |
| **Hosting** | DigitalOcean, AWS Lightsail, or Hetzner | Cost-effective VPS; managed database add-on |
| **Backups** | Automated daily database backups + object storage versioning | Critical for user data and listings |
| **Monitoring** | Laravel Telescope (dev) + Sentry (error tracking) + UptimeRobot (availability) | Free tiers sufficient for MVP |

### 20.2 Key Architectural Decisions

1. **Monolith first, services later.** A single Laravel application handles all modules. Do not build microservices for an MVP. Split when scaling demands it.

2. **Web-first, mobile-second.** The web application is mobile-responsive. Drivers and students use it in the browser. This avoids app store approval delays, forces a simpler UI, and reaches users on any device. Build React Native apps only after web traction is proven.

3. **API-first internally.** Even though the MVP uses Blade, build the backend logic as API endpoints (Laravel routes returning JSON) and have Blade consume them. This makes the future mobile app a thin client over the same API.

4. **LATRA integration from day one.** The ride-hailing license requires API integration for trip data export . Design the trip table with all required fields (Trip ID, coordinates, timestamps, fare, distance, rating, driver earning, license number, vehicle registration) from the start.

5. **No in-house payment processing.** Integrate with a payment aggregator (Selcom, Flutterwave, or direct M-Pesa API). The platform records transactions and calculates commissions; it does not hold customer funds. This avoids Bank of Tanzania payment system provider licensing requirements.

### 21.3 Key Database Constraints and Indexing

- **Foreign keys** on all relationships with `ON DELETE RESTRICT` (prevent accidental data loss).
- **Unique constraints:** `users.phone`, `vehicles.plate_number`, `universities.short_name`.
- **Indexes:** `driver_profiles.university_id + status` (for driver discovery), `properties.university_id + status` (for property listing), `ride_bookings.student_id`, `ride_bookings.driver_id`, `accommodation_inquiries.student_id`.
- **Spatial (future):** PostGIS `GEOGRAPHY` column for driver and property locations; `ST_DWithin` for radius queries.
- **Transaction management:** Use database transactions for all booking creation flows to prevent partial writes.

### 21.4 Double-Booking Prevention (Accommodation)

```sql
-- Pseudo-logic within a transaction
BEGIN;
SELECT * FROM bookable_units WHERE id = ? FOR UPDATE;
-- Check for overlapping bookings
SELECT 1 FROM accommodation_bookings 
WHERE bookable_unit_id = ? 
AND status IN ('confirmed', 'pending')
AND (start_date <= ? AND end_date >= ?);
-- If no overlap: INSERT booking; COMMIT
-- If overlap: ROLLBACK; return error
```


## 22. UI/UX Strategy

### 22.1 Design Principles

1. **Mobile-first.** Design for a 360px-wide screen. Most students will access via phone. Desktop is secondary.
2. **Low-bandwidth tolerance.** Use compressed images, lazy loading, skeleton screens, and clear loading states. Avoid heavy JavaScript frameworks for the MVP.
3. **Swahili-first for drivers.** Driver-facing interfaces should prioritize Swahili (with English toggle). Student interfaces can be bilingual.
4. **Trust signals everywhere.** Verified badges, review counts, response times, and clear pricing reduce anxiety in both marketplaces.
5. **One primary action per screen.** Students should never be confused about what to do next.

### 22.2 Screen Descriptions

| Screen | Key Elements | Design Notes |
|--------|-------------|--------------|
| **Public Homepage** | University selector (prominent), UNIRIDE intro with sample availability, UNISTAY intro with sample properties, login/register CTA | The university selector is the hero element. Show live-ish data (“12 drivers available near UDOM”) to demonstrate liquidity. |
| **Registration/Login** | Phone number + OTP (primary), email optional, role selection (Student/Driver/Owner) | Phone-first because that’s the identity anchor in Tanzania. OTP via SMS. |

## 23. Security and Legal Requirements

### 23.1 Mandatory Legal Requirements (Verified)

| Requirement | Authority | Status | Action |
|-------------|-----------|--------|--------|
| **Personal Data Protection registration** | PDPC | Mandatory before collecting personal data  | Register as data controller and data processor. Appoint a Data Protection Officer (DPO) if processing sensitive data at scale. |
| **Ride Hailing Service Operator License** | LATRA | Mandatory before operating ride-hailing  | Complete Stage 1 (app test), Stage 2 (API integration), Stage 3 (document submission). Annual license fee applies. |
| **Vehicle Tracking Devices** | LATRA | Required for ride-sharing vehicles  | Clarify with LATRA whether smartphone GPS satisfies for bodaboda; if not, require approved hardware. |
| **LATRA Levy Collection** | LATRA | Platform must calculate, collect, remit levies  | Build levy calculation into fare model. |
| **Business Registration** | BRELA | Mandatory | Register company (Ltd) with object clause covering platform services. |
| **Tax Registration (TIN, VAT)** | TRA | Mandatory | TIN required for business. VAT registration if turnover exceeds threshold. |
| **Local Government Service Levy** | Municipal Council | Mandatory | Register business with local council; pay service levy. |

### 23.2 Recommended Security Practices (Not Explicitly Mandated but Essential)

- **Authentication:** Phone OTP for all users. Password optional (some users may prefer OTP-only login). Session management with secure, HTTP-only cookies.
- **Authorization:** Role-based middleware. Students cannot access driver routes; drivers cannot access admin routes.
- **Driver Verification:** Require national ID, driving license, vehicle registration, and insurance. Verify against LATRA vehicle API where possible . Manual admin review for MVP.
- **Property Verification:** Require ownership documents (title deed, rental agreement, or utility bill in owner’s name). Admin review with document upload. Display “Verified” badge only after review.
- **Location Privacy:** Never expose exact coordinates of students or drivers in public views. Use approximate area names (“Near UDOM Main Gate”). Exact location shared only between matched student-driver pair during active trip.
- **Payment Security:** Do not store card details. Use payment provider’s hosted checkout or API. Log all transactions. Reconcile daily.
- **Fraud Prevention:** Rate-limit booking requests. Flag accounts with high cancellation rates. Monitor for fake listings (manual review + user reports). Require phone verification before listing.
- **Data Backup:** Automated daily database backups with 30-day retention. Test restore procedure monthly.
- **Admin Accountability:** All admin actions logged with timestamp, admin ID, and action details. No shared admin accounts.

### 23.3 Areas Requiring Professional Legal Advice

- **LATRA license application specifics:** The exact fare framework, levy percentage, and vehicle tracking device requirements for motorcycles (bodaboda) need clarification from LATRA directly.
- **PDPC registration category:** Whether UNIRIDE UNISTAY is a data controller, data processor, or both, and the applicable fee schedule.
- **Payment aggregator agreement:** The legal structure for commission collection and remittance. Whether the platform can hold funds in escrow or must use a split-payment model.
- **Consumer protection:** Liability for ride safety incidents, accommodation misrepresentation, and refund policies.
- **Employment classification:** Whether drivers are independent contractors or employees under Tanzanian law.

## 28. Final Recommendations

1. **Launch UNISTAY first at UDOM.** It has no LATRA requirement, builds the student用户 base, and validates the university-filtering model. Use a free listing period to acquire property owners, then introduce a success fee.

2. **Secure LATRA clarification before building UNIRIDE.** The vehicle tracking device requirement for motorcycles is a potential blocker. Confirm with LATRA whether smartphone GPS is acceptable for bodaboda, or whether approved hardware is mandatory. Do not build the ride-hailing module until this is resolved.

3. **Register with PDPC before collecting any user data.** This is non-negotiable and can be done in parallel with development. Budget for the registration fee and appoint a responsible person for data protection.

4. **Build web-first, mobile-responsive.** Do not spend time and money on native apps until the web platform has proven product-market fit. A Laravel + Livewire application with PostgreSQL is sufficient and fast to develop.

5. **Solve the cold-start problem with a single-campus focus.** Density matters more than reach. 50 active drivers at one university is more valuable than 500 scattered across ten universities. Dominate one campus, then replicate.

6. **Do not become a payment provider.** Integrate with M-Pesa/Mixx/Airtel Money as a merchant. Record transactions, calculate commissions, but let the licensed providers handle fund custody. This avoids TZS 500 million minimum capital requirements and complex BOT licensing.

7. **Price for student reality.** Students are price-sensitive. A 12% ride commission and 5–7% accommodation success fee are competitive against informal alternatives (negotiated bodaboda fares, dalali fees). Keep the platform free for students.

8. **Invest in verification, not marketing, in the first 6 months.** Trust is the platform’s core product. A verified listing is worth more than ten unverified ones. Manual verification at low volume is acceptable and builds quality standards.

9. **Plan for seasonality.** Student demand fluctuates with the academic calendar: high at semester start (accommodation search, move-in transport), lower during exams, near-zero during holidays. Build cash reserves for low periods.

10. **Keep the regulatory file current.** LATRA and PDPC requirements can change. Budget for legal counsel and compliance monitoring. A lapsed license or data protection violation can shut down operations.
